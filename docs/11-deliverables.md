**Writing rules:**

- No number is typed by hand. Scripts write `reports/results.json`, and the report pulls its values from that file (LaTeX macros, or a find-and-replace step).
- Claims respect the minimum detectable effect; smaller differences are called inconclusive.
- Every deviation from the proposal is stated once, in Method or Setup, with its reason (from [decisions.md](decisions.md)).
- Attribution to Open Food Facts, and the disclaimer, appear in the paper.

### 3.4 Likely questions

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Why not just ask an LLM? | Retrievers and LLMs fail on negation and numeric conditions (NevIR, ExcluIR, NumConQ). LLM allergy advice has been shown to be unreliable. We need deterministic, checkable constraints, and generated explanations can be unfaithful. |
| How do you know the allergen data is right? | We don't, and we say so. PASS means "not declared in this record". Traces fail, unknown is excluded, and the audit (S2) measured the error rate. |
| Isn't coverage too low if you reject unknowns? | Here is the coverage number and its cost curve. A false "safe" is far worse than a missing result. OFF's own preference matching makes the same distinction. |
| How did you choose the weights? | A grid on 12 dev scenarios, never on held-out, with a sensitivity plot. |
| Are 20 scenarios enough? | For large effects, yes. We state the minimum detectable effect and call smaller differences inconclusive. |
| Why is BM25 still a strong baseline? | The TREC 2023 Product Search track found that lexical systems beat single-stage dense retrieval on product search. |
| Why RRF? | It needs no labels and no score calibration. We also tested the convex combination that Bruch et al. found stronger. |
| How big is it, and how fast? | Corpus size and latency per stage from the RQ6 table. |
| Can I use this data? | Yes, under ODbL with attribution to Open Food Facts. Our test collection is released under the same terms. |
